  • Safety hazard identified for ortho-carborane mixtures Science
  • A new publication in Inorganic Chemistry warns that ortho-carborane—a boron-rich cage cluster widely explored in medicinal chemistry and boron neutron capture therapy (BNCT)—poses a significant, previously unrecognized explosion hazard when mixed with a variety of common laboratory materials.

  • Ortho-carboranes are prized in oncology drug discovery for their high boron-10 (¹⁰B) neutron cross-section, which captures low-energy neutrons to emit high-energy α-particles that destroy localized tumor tissue.

  • Large-scale genomic analysis identifies novel risk genes for neurodegenerative diseases Research Preprint

  • A population-scale gene-burden testing analysis of rare damaging coding variants identified IMPA2 as a novel risk gene associated with Alzheimer's disease and related dementias (ADRD).

  • The study also uncovered novel Parkinson's disease and related disorders (PDRD) associations at ANKRD27 and CCL7, with the strongest signal at ANKRD27 clustering within domains that mediate interactions with Rab GTPases and retromer components.

  • Rare human MYCBP2 variant enhances memory persistence across species Research Preprint

  • Whole-exome sequencing of an individual with highly superior autobiographical memory (HSAM) identified a rare, de novo MYCBP2 V768F missense variant affecting an evolutionarily conserved residue in the encoded E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase.
  • In vivo engineering of the homologous substitution in C. elegans and heterozygous knock-in mice improved long-term memory retention without affecting baseline sensory, motor, or growth phenotypes.

  • Selective prime editing of the variant in the dorsal hippocampal neurons of adult mice was sufficient to enhance spatial long-term memory, demonstrating that its cognitive effects do not require developmental expression.

  • Single-cell profiling reveals placental alterations in PMOS non-responsive to metformin Research Preprint

  • Multimodal single-cell molecular analysis of placentas from pregnancies with polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS) revealed cell-type-specific transcriptional downregulation and altered cell-type proportions.
  • Syncytiotrophoblasts exhibited reduced growth hormone receptor signaling activity, while endothelial cells showed diminished receptor tyrosine kinase pathway activity (including VEGFC) despite increased hypervascularity.

  • Metformin treatment induced minimal molecular improvements in PMOS placentas, with its associated transcriptional shifts linked to lower birth weight and higher childhood BMI.

  • Calmodulin mutation N54I drives CPVT in a novel heterozygous mouse model Research Preprint

  • A heterozygous mouse model carrying the human disease-associated Calm1 N54I mutation showed that mutant calmodulin, which accounted for just 13.7% of total cardiac calmodulin, is sufficient to drive arrhythmogenic phenotypes.

  • Isolated cardiomyocytes showed increased rates of RyR2-mediated spontaneous calcium release, while conscious mutant mice exhibited significant ventricular ectopy, ventricular tachycardia, and atrial electrical abnormalities under catecholaminergic or exercise challenges.

  • Randomized controlled trial of video game therapeutic Meliora shows no increase in gaming-related problems Research Preprint

  • A three-arm randomized controlled trial of 1,001 adults with major depressive disorder (MDD) evaluated the long-term adjunctive use of the video game therapeutic Meliora.
  • Over 12 weeks, Game Addiction Scale (GAS-7) scores decreased significantly in the Meliora group (mean change, -0.63 points), showing a greater reduction compared to a sham video game control (mean change, -0.02; p = 0.001) and similar outcomes to treatment-as-usual (TAU).
  • The results suggest that long-term use of this digital therapeutic does not promote gaming addiction or related behavioral issues.

  • Anti-seizure drug levetiracetam targets diffuse midline glioma — Nature Medicine
  • Levetiracetam selectively dampens GABAergic synaptic signaling, leading to reduced tumor growth and prolonged survival in experimental models and patient cohorts of diffuse midline glioma.

  • This therapeutic effect is specific to midline gliomas (not hemispheric high-grade gliomas) and operates independently of the drug's canonical SV2A-mediated primary anti-seizure mechanism.

  • Manganese availability regulates Akt activity and insulin sensitivity — bioRxiv/medRxiv

  • Hepatocyte manganese (Mn) concentrations act as a key determinant of PI3K-independent Akt function in vivo, with Mn levels fluctuating during fasting and feeding via the transporter SLC30A10.

  • Mn increases Akt's catalytic efficiency, acting additively with insulin to enhance Akt activity and suppress hepatic glucose output.

  • Scn9a inhibition protects pancreatic beta cells in type 1 diabetes — bioRxiv/medRxiv

  • Pharmacological or genetic knockout of the Na⁺ channel β-subunit gene Scn9a (Nav1.7) in NOD mice significantly reduced β-cell Na⁺ currents, Ca²⁺ responses, and insulin secretion under high-glucose conditions.

  • The sodium channel inhibitor carbamazepine protects human and mouse β-cell survival in vitro and in vivo, and its protective pathway is mediated through Scn9a.

  • Large-scale oncology pharmacogenomics platform maps systemic toxicity determinants — bioRxiv/medRxiv

  • Researchers developed MSK-Tox, an LLM-based pan-cancer resource capturing clinical toxicity events (pneumonitis, liver toxicity, colitis, etc.) across more than 50,000 patients.

  • The analysis identified a germline regulatory variant near FOXE1 that confers a high risk of thyroid toxicity across multiple systemic therapy regimens.

  • Genetic variant drives 25-fold higher lung cancer risk in never-smokers — STAT News

  • A newly identified, highly rare genetic variant has been associated with a 25-fold increase in the odds of developing lung cancer among individuals who have never smoked.

  • [Biotech] Laromestrocel stem cell therapy preserves brain volume in mild Alzheimer's — bioRxiv/medRxiv

  • A Phase I/II trial (N=49) of the mesenchymal stem cell therapy laromestrocel in mild Alzheimer's disease demonstrated significant inhibition of progressive brain inflammation.
  • Compared to placebo (N=9), subjects receiving monthly doses of 25 million (25M) or 100 million (100M) cells over 39 weeks showed reduced neuroinflammation in the left hippocampus (p < 0.001) and reduced plasma neurogranin (p = 0.023).

  • Decreased neuroinflammation correlated directly with reduced brain atrophy (R = -0.326, p = 0.040) and improved MoCA cognitive scores (R = -0.401, p = 0.011).

  • Closed-loop auditory stimulation during sleep improves memory in early Alzheimer's — bioRxiv/medRxiv

  • A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial (N=37) in patients with biomarker-confirmed early symptomatic Alzheimer's tested one night of closed-loop auditory stimulation (CLAS) of slow sleep oscillations.

  • CLAS significantly improved overnight declarative memory retention (word-pair consolidation, Cohen's dz = 0.42, p = 0.04) and enhanced slow-wave and fast spindle electrophysiological activity.
